# LinkedIn drafts: AI-marker checklist

Applies to: every LinkedIn draft (personal profile and Sicknote page), its first comment, and all slide text.
Source basis: Wikipedia "Signs of AI writing" (WikiProject AI Cleanup), compiled from two extractions of that guide. Wikipedia-only items (wiki markup, citation templates, article sections) are left out because they cannot occur in a LinkedIn post.

## Rule zero: substance before scrubbing

The guide warns that these markers are symptoms. Removing them from empty text only makes empty text harder to spot. Every draft must carry at least one thing a generic AI could not write:

- something Viktor saw or learned as a GP (de-identified), or
- a Sicknote data point (aggregate only), or
- a specific cited study with its real limits stated.

A draft with none of these is rejected before the marker check runs.

## 1. Inflated significance

Reject:
- stands as / serves as / is a testament to / is a reminder of
- plays a vital / crucial / pivotal / key role
- a pivotal moment, a key turning point, marks a shift, represents a shift
- underscores / highlights the importance of
- reflects a broader trend, part of a broader movement
- setting the stage for, shaping the future of, evolving landscape
- enduring legacy, indelible mark, deeply rooted, rich heritage
- "more than just a [thing]"

Fix: say what happened and why it matters, in plain words, with a number or example.

## 2. Promotional and ad-like language

Reject: boasts, vibrant, rich, profound, groundbreaking, game-changer, renowned, showcasing, exemplifies, commitment to, nestled, in the heart of, diverse array, featuring, seamless, hassle-free, unlock, harness, empower, elevate, transform your, "we're excited to announce", "book now".

Fix: describe the mechanism or the concrete benefit. "Takes 10 minutes from a phone" beats "a seamless experience".

## 3. Tacked-on "analysis" at the end of sentences

Reject trailing participle clauses that add nothing:
- ", highlighting its significance"
- ", reflecting its continued relevance"
- ", contributing to better outcomes"
- ", further enhancing patient care"
- ", demonstrating the ongoing importance"
- ", creating a more efficient system"

Also reject unsupported claims like "has sparked debate about" or "prompted wider reflection on" unless a named source shows it.

Fix: delete the clause, or make it its own sentence with evidence.

## 4. Vague attributions and weasel words

Reject: experts say, studies show, research suggests, some argue, critics argue, industry reports suggest, observers note, it is widely understood that, it is important to note that, several sources.

Fix: name the study, author, journal or body (HSE, NICE, ICGP, CDC), or delete the claim.

## 5. False balance and fence-sitting

Reject "on the one hand / on the other hand" and "while X has benefits, Y also raises valid points" when Viktor actually holds a view.

Fix: take the position. Say what you think and why. Admit uncertainty only where it is real, and say what it is.

## 6. Formula endings and summaries

Reject: In conclusion, In summary, Overall, Ultimately, At the end of the day, "Despite these challenges, X continues to...", "The future of X lies in its ability to...", "Only time will tell".

Fix: end on the strongest specific point or a real question Viktor wants answered.

## 7. Covering knowledge gaps

Reject: "while specific details are limited", "based on available information", "not widely documented", "as of my last update".

Fix: say plainly what is not known, or leave the point out.

## 8. High-density AI vocabulary

Reject in drafts (no exceptions, since the rule is zero markers):
additionally (as an opener), align with, bolster, crucial, delve, demystify, enduring, enhance, foster, garner, highlight (as a verb), interplay, intricate, key (as an adjective), landscape (abstract), masterclass, meticulous, navigate (metaphorical), pivotal, realm, resonate, robust, showcase, tailored, tapestry, testament, underscore, valuable, vibrant, multifaceted, nuanced, moreover, furthermore, in today's fast-paced world.

## 9. Avoiding plain "is" and "has"

Reject: serves as, stands as, functions as, represents (meaning "is"), features (meaning "has"), offers (meaning "has").

Fix: use "is" and "has".

## 10. Negative parallelisms

Reject:
- "It's not X, it's Y" / "This isn't about X. It's about Y."
- "Not only X but also Y"
- "No X. No Y. Just Z."

Fix: state the point directly.

## 11. Rule of three

Reject stacked triplets: three adjectives in a row, three benefits, three takeaways, where the count is forced.

Fix: use the real number of points. Two is fine. One is often better.

## 12. Synonym cycling

Reject swapping synonyms for the same thing in back-to-back sentences (sick note, medical certificate, fit note, doctor's note within one paragraph).

Fix: pick one term and repeat it. Use the term the reader's country uses: "sick cert" in Ireland, "fit note" in the UK.

## 13. False ranges

Reject "from X to Y" when X and Y are not two ends of a real scale ("from small clinics to global health systems").

Fix: list the actual items.

## 14. Forced numbering

Reject "3 pillars of", "5 key strategies", "the 4 Cs of" unless the content really has exactly that many parts.

## 15. Formatting tells

- Em dashes: none in post text. Use commas, full stops or brackets.
- En dashes for ranges, not hyphens: 2024–2026, 3–5 days.
- Bold: none in post text (LinkedIn has no native bold; unicode bold is also a tell).
- No "Term: definition" bullet lists with bold headers.
- No emoji in list bullets or as section markers. At most one emoji per post, only if Viktor would use it.
- Hashtags: 0–3, at the end, never inside sentences.
- No title case in slide headings. Use sentence case.
- Straight quotes in post text.

## 16. Chatbot leftovers

Reject anything addressed to the drafter or the user: "Here's a draft", "I hope this helps", "Certainly!", "Feel free to adjust", placeholders like [Name] or [insert stat], and any "As an AI" text.

## 17. LinkedIn-specific AI tells

Not in the Wikipedia guide, but the same failure in LinkedIn form:
- Hooks: "Here's the thing", "Let that sink in", "Read that again", "Unpopular opinion:", "I'll say it louder", "Nobody talks about this"
- Engagement bait endings: "Agree?", "Thoughts?", "Drop a comment below", "Repost if you agree"
- "I'm humbled / thrilled to share"
- One-sentence paragraphs used for every single line (some short lines are fine; every line is a tell)
- Cliffhanger "Here's why 👇"

## How the check runs

1. Draft is written from Viktor's voice guide.
2. Automated scan: every phrase and word above is searched. Any hit sends the draft back for rewrite.
3. Separate reviewer pass (not the drafter) reads for the patterns a word scan cannot catch: inflated significance, tacked-on analysis, false balance, forced triplets, synonym cycling, and rule zero.
4. Only drafts that pass both reach Metricool as drafts for Viktor's approval.
